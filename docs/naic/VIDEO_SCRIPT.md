# TradeVoice demo video script (NAIC 2026)

**What NAIC asks for:** 3 to 5 minutes "showing the build working end to end, ideally with a real user speaking a
Nigerian language".
**Our plan:** about 4 minutes 55 seconds. A real trader speaks Yorùbá (or Hausa) into the live app. A team member
narrates in English. Every Yorùbá, Hausa, Igbo and Pidgin line has English subtitles. If the edit runs over 5:00,
shorten scenes 2 and 8 first.

**People on camera**
- **Trader:** [trader's first name, only with written or recorded consent], a real trader who speaks [Yorùbá / Hausa].
- **Narrator:** [team member], voice over in English.
- **Second speaker (Telegram scene):** [team member or trader who speaks Igbo or Pidgin].

**Customers in the demo are made up:** Iya Bisi, Mama Ngozi, Oga Emeka. Use a fresh test account, never a pilot
trader's real book.

**Before filming:** read the filming notes at the end of this file.

## The script

### 1. The problem (0:00-0:20)
- **On screen:** the trader's stall in the market. Close-up of a worn notebook: names, amounts, lines crossed out.
  Hands turning the pages. (Film a notebook page written for the video with made-up names, not a real one.)
- **Narrator:** "A market trader sells on credit all day. 'Take it, pay me Friday.' Those debts live in a notebook,
  or in her head. Some are forgotten. And when she asks a lender for a loan, she has no records to show."

### 2. TradeVoice in one line (0:20-0:35)
- **On screen:** the TradeVoice logo and "Records that speak your language". Then the website,
  tradevoice.duckdns.org, on a phone.
- **Narrator:** "TradeVoice keeps the book for her. She talks, types or photographs her notebook, in English,
  Pidgin, Yorùbá, Hausa or Igbo. The AI reads the words. Code does every sum."

### 3. Sign up on the web app (0:35-0:55)
- **On screen:** screen recording of the phone. tradevoice.duckdns.org/app, pick **Yorùbá**, tap
  **Create account**. Type the number (**blur it in the edit**), tick the terms. If the SMS code screen appears,
  show the code arriving (blur the number). Create a password. Enter a name and a business name. Tap
  **Create my account**.
- **Narrator:** "Sign-up is a phone number and a password. When our SMS phone is on, a 6-digit code comes by SMS.
  No long forms, no card."

### 4. Live talk in Yorùbá: a credit sale (0:55-1:35)
- **On screen:** the trader at her stall, phone in hand. She taps the mic. The orb follows her voice and her words
  appear on screen as she talks. Then TradeVoice answers out loud.
- **Trader (Yorùbá):** "Mo ta àpò ìrẹsì 2 fún Iya Bisi ní 60000, gbèsè ni, yóò san lọ́jọ́ Ẹtì."
  - **Subtitle:** "I sold 2 bags of rice to Iya Bisi for 60,000. It is on credit. She will pay on Friday."
- **Hausa alternative:** "Na sayar da buhun shinkafa 2 ga Iya Bisi 60000, bashi ne, za ta biya ranar Juma'a."
  - **Subtitle:** the same as above.
- **TradeVoice (out loud, in her language):** reads the record back and asks whether to save it.
  - **Subtitle:** [write down what TradeVoice actually said in the take, in English].
- **Narrator (over the end of the scene):** "She speaks the way she would tell a friend. Live talk hears her as she
  talks, and N-ATLaS writes the record."
- Both lines are adapted from the team's test sentences (`eval/cases_lang.jsonl`). The trader should say it in her
  own words. A native speaker checks the subtitle against what she really said.

### 5. The check card and Save (1:35-1:55)
- **On screen:** tap to check it on screen. The card shows ₦60,000, sold on credit, Iya Bisi, rice, 2 bags,
  pay by Friday. If any field says **Check this**, keep it in the shot. She taps **Save** (or says "Bẹ́ẹ̀ni" in
  Yorùbá, "Eh" in Hausa: yes). A "Saved" message shows what Iya Bisi now owes.
- **Narrator:** "Nothing is saved until she says yes. The amount must be one she said. Totals and balances are
  worked out by code, never by the AI."

### 6. "Who owes me?" (1:55-2:15)
- **On screen:** she asks out loud (live talk is still listening; if it has stopped, she taps the mic again).
  TradeVoice answers out loud. Cut to Home: **To collect**, with Iya Bisi and the amount.
- **Trader (Yorùbá):** "Ta ló jẹ mí lówó?"
  - **Subtitle:** "Who owes me money?"
- **Hausa alternative:** "Su wa ke da bashina?"
  - **Subtitle:** "Who owes me?"
- **TradeVoice (out loud):** names and amounts from her book.
  - **Subtitle:** [what TradeVoice actually said, in English].
- **Narrator:** "The answer comes from her own book, so it is never a guess."

### 7. A reminder the trader sends (2:15-2:35)
- **On screen:** **Customers**, then Iya Bisi, then **Prepare reminder**. The sheet says "TradeVoice prepared this.
  You send it." She taps **Send on WhatsApp**: her own WhatsApp opens with the message ready, and she sends it.
  Use a team phone saved as "Iya Bisi" as the receiver, and **blur every phone number** in WhatsApp.
- **Narrator:** "TradeVoice drafts the reminder in her language. She sends it from her own WhatsApp. TradeVoice never
  messages her customers."

### 8. A notebook photo (2:35-3:00)
- **On screen:** Home, **Scan book**, **Choose photo**. She photographs a notebook page written for the video, for
  example: "Mama Ngozi 3 crates eggs 13,500 credit", "Oga Emeka paid 10,000", "Transport 2,000". TradeVoice shows
  the lines it found, with amber lines to look at. She ticks them and taps **Save ticked**.
- **Narrator:** "Old pages count too. A vision model reads the lines, N-ATLaS turns them into records, and she checks
  each line before it is saved."

### 9. The same on Telegram (3:00-3:30)
- **On screen:** Telegram on a phone. The TradeVoice bot asks to **Share my phone number**; tap it (blur the
  number). The first time, the bot also asks for a language and for agreement: pick Igbo (or English for Pidgin).
  Then a voice note. Use the Telegram account of the same number that made the web account, so it is the same
  book.
- **Second speaker (Igbo):** "Iya Bisi akwụọla 20000 nke ọ ji m."
  - **Subtitle:** "Iya Bisi has paid 20,000 of what she owes me."
- **Pidgin alternative:** "Iya Bisi don pay 20,000 from the money wey she owe me."
  - **Subtitle:** the same as above.
- **On screen:** the bot replies in text and with a voice note, with **Yes, save** and **No** buttons. Tap
  **Yes, save**. Cut to the web app: Iya Bisi's balance has changed in the same book.
  - **Subtitle for the bot's voice reply:** [what the bot actually said, in English].
- **Narrator:** "Many traders prefer to chat. On Telegram, TradeVoice works the same way: voice notes, photos,
  buttons and spoken replies, in the same book. It is free. Our WhatsApp bot is built and tested, but it is not
  live yet: we could not get WhatsApp Business API access in time."

### 10. N-ATLaS explained (3:30-4:20)
- **On screen:** a simple diagram: voice note, then N-ATLaS speech models, then N-ATLaS language model, then code
  checks, then the trader's book. Then the benchmark table from `docs/RESULTS.md` (1,000 sentences), one row at a
  time.
- **Narrator:** "Under the hood is N-ATLaS. Its four speech models hear voice notes in Yorùbá, Hausa, Igbo and
  Nigerian-accented English. Traders mix languages, so a Yorùbá, Hausa or Igbo note is heard twice, and N-ATLaS
  merges the two. In live talk, Intron's streaming speech hears and speaks, so the words appear at once. Either way,
  the N-ATLaS language model turns the words into a record.
  On 1,000 test sentences, N-ATLaS alone got 69% right. Llama-3 8B, the model it was built from, got 51%. In Hausa,
  67% against 27%. For amounts said in local number words, 62% against 12%. Wrong amounts: 68 against 170.
  Alone, neither model is safe for money. With our code checks, the full app scored 100%, with no wrong amounts.
  These were test sentences, not traders' own words, so the pilot is the real test."
- **On-screen numbers (all from `docs/RESULTS.md`, 2 Oct 2026 run):**

  | | N-ATLaS alone | Llama-3-8B alone | Full app |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | All | 69% | 51% | 100% |
  | Hausa | 67% | 27% | 100% |
  | Local number words | 62% | 12% | 100% |
  | Wrong amounts | 68 | 170 | 0 |

### 11. Pilot results (4:20-4:40)
- **On screen:** the validation report (`scripts/validation_report.py`): the "At a glance" tiles, then
  conversations per day.
- **Narrator:** "From [pilot start date] to [pilot end date], [traders] traders used TradeVoice in [languages]. They
  had [conversations] conversations and saved [records saved] records. N-ATLaS gave [N-ATLaS share] of the AI
  answers. Because of them, we changed [one change from the report]."
- Fill every bracket from the validation report. If a number is small, say it as it is.

### 12. Close (4:40-4:55)
- **On screen:** the trader at her stall, smiling or back at work. Then a plain end card:
  - TradeVoice: Records that speak your language
  - tradevoice.duckdns.org
  - **Attribution, on screen for at least 5 seconds:** "N-ATLaS is an initiative of the Federal Ministry of
    Communications, Innovation and Digital Economy, and powered by Awarri Technologies."
- **Narrator:** "TradeVoice. Records that speak your language."

## Filming notes
**Consent and privacy**
- Any real trader on camera gives consent first, read to them in their language: what the video is for, that NAIC
  and the public may see it, and that they can say no or stop at any time. Keep the signed form or recorded "yes"
  in the team's private folder, never in GitHub.
- Customer names are made up (Iya Bisi, Mama Ngozi, Oga Emeka). Never film a real customer's name or a real
  notebook page.
- Never show a real phone number: blur it at sign-up, in WhatsApp and in Telegram. Never show a key, a token or a
  `/team?key=` link. Turn on Do Not Disturb so no message pops up.

**Takes**
- Film the main take at the market, then a backup take of scenes 4 to 6 in a quiet place, with the same lines. If
  market noise spoils the main take, use the quiet one.
- Open the app a few minutes before filming so N-ATLaS is awake. If you cut a wait in the edit, keep the result as
  it was. Never edit what TradeVoice heard, wrote or said.
- Film the real live app. No mock-ups.

**Subtitles and editing**
- English subtitles for every Yorùbá, Hausa, Igbo and Pidgin line, burned into the video so they always show.
- A native speaker checks each subtitle against what was really said.
- Keep the video between 3 and 5 minutes. Export at 1080p.
- Put the attribution in the video description too.

**Upload**
- Upload to YouTube as **unlisted**, or to Google Drive with "anyone with the link" can view.
- Test the link in a private, logged-out browser window before submitting.
- Put the link in the NAIC form and in [`CHECKLIST.md`](CHECKLIST.md).
